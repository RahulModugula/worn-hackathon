# Storytelling research for the pitch (read 2026-11-21)

Secondary sources are marked. Nothing here is a promise that a story wins a room: published effect sizes for narrative persuasion are small.

## What the evidence supports
- **One concrete character, one clear problem, a demo that shows a real outcome, a closing callback.** Narrative transportation predicts story-consistent beliefs (Green & Brock 2000, J. Personality & Social Psychology 79:701-721). A meta-analysis found small average narrative effects, about r = .17-.19 (Braddock & Dillard 2016, Communication Monographs 83(4), via secondary summaries; verify before citing).
- **Identifiable victim effect is small** (r = .05 across 41 studies, Lee & Feeley 2016, publication-bias caveats). **Numbers placed next to an identifiable case reduced giving** (Small, Loewenstein & Slovic 2007, OBHDP 102:143-153). **Implication: story first, then ONE number, never stacked.**
- **Made to Stick (Heath & Heath):** simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, stories (secondary summary only).
- **Resonate (Duarte 2010):** alternate "what is" with "what could be" (secondary).
- **YC demo-day guidance:** say plainly what you do, do not bury the lead, listeners keep at most 3-4 points (ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches, 2016).

## Live-demo risk (sourced)
- Meta Connect 2025 live glasses demos failed on stage; the explanation changed from Wi-Fi to traffic overload (uploadvr.com, 2025). **Lesson: recorded and offline fallbacks, explain failures plainly, never blame the network first.**

## Healthcare pitfalls (sourced)
- Overclaiming and implied endorsements sank Theranos (imd.org, mofo.com analyses, 2026-11-21). **Do not overclaim outcomes; do not use logos that imply endorsement; never use a real patient.**
- Composite characters must be labelled. **We have no interviews, so Priya is not a composite: she is "a fictional nurse in our synthetic demo".** Say it on a slide and out loud.

## Not verified (treat as practitioner convention, say nothing about "studies")
First-10-seconds techniques, handing the device to a reviewer, silence and eye contact, the exact wording of an ask.

## Nurse voices and statistics we can quote (read 2026-11-21; several pages were behind paywalls, so marked "snippet")
- **Nurse quote, 2025 ethnography:** "we have to get care to our patients" (Debono et al., Frontiers in Health Services, 2025; 91 shifts, 46 interviews, Australia). Overdue-medication alerts made nurses feel like "personal failures" (same paper). frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frhs.2025.1579265/full
- **Interruptions during medication passes:** 53.1% of 4,271 observed administrations were interrupted; each interruption was linked to a 12.1% rise in procedural failures and a 12.7% rise in clinical errors (Westbrook et al., Arch Intern Med 2010; observational, 2006-08 data; via AHRQ PSNet).
- **Charting vs patients:** 79% of acute-care nurses lose time each week to charting they call unproductive, 34% spend 3+ hours a week on it (KLAS/Arch 2025, n=80,147; vendor-community survey, "unproductive" is self-defined; Becker's snippet).
- **Turnover cost:** $60,090 per bedside RN (NSI 2026 report, read directly; NSI 2025 edition said $61,110).
- **Presence, not paperwork:** Corewell Health's CNIO says the aim is more capacity "to be present with patients"; the nurse pilot reports ~28 minutes saved per shift at ~60% use (vendor-reported, not independently evaluated; snippet).
- **Hands-free interest:** EMS providers shown smart-glasses concepts said it "would save so much time for the patient" and worried recordings could be used "against you" (Zhang et al., JMIR Human Factors 2022, n=13). **Design implication: privacy by design, no recordings stored.** An earlier small Google Glass wound-care pilot (16 nurses, PLoS ONE 2015) found barcode patient ID a preferred feature.
- **Patients:** small qualitative studies report patients want engagement while a nurse uses technology; one describes a "game of tag" between patient and computer (snippets only).
- **Wrong-patient risk, aggregate only:** an analysis of 826 wrong-patient event reports (Pennsylvania Patient Safety Authority, 2011) found inadequate patient identification checks in 12.9%. Use aggregates, do not retell named cases (Josie King is an order-communication case, family is public; not recommended for a hackathon pitch).
- **Not found:** a verified nurse quote about charting after the shift ("pajama time"); do not claim it.

### Hooks fully backed by the sources above (pick one)
1. "Every interruption during a medication pass was linked to more errors, and more than half of passes were interrupted." (Westbrook 2010)
2. A nurse in Australia: "we have to get care to our patients." (Debono 2025)
3. "Nurses say they want to be present with patients, not at a screen." (Corewell CNIO)
4. "Eight in ten acute-care nurses lose time every week to charting they call unproductive." (KLAS 2025, say "survey")
5. "Losing one nurse costs a hospital about $60,000." (NSI 2026)

## Hackathon format note (unverified secondary)
Short pitches (~30 s) and ~5-minute demos are common; the Worn schedule says table demos then finalists 3 min + 2 min Q&A (worn-hacks.com, 2026-11-21).
